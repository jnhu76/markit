## Reading notes

- **Granularity is not the family boundary.** M3/M4 reuse reaches individual
  nodes and whole blocks; M5 reuses arbitrary subtrees by position; M7
  reuses only two node kinds. What separates H2 from H3 in the evidence is
  the lookup/vouching strategy (fragment window + live parse state vs free
  consultation of the edited old tree), and what separates H4-shaped
  behavior is who owns the convergence decision (dedicated checkpoints M7,
  state agreement inside reuse M5, theorem matchpoints M8).
- **Convergence authorities differ.** M8 validates against batch-parser
  configurations; M7 validates byte-locally per checkpoint; M5 validates
  parser/scanner-state agreement at splice points; M4 validates
  Markdown block-context hashes. No extracted mechanism implements the
  literal shape "reparse, then compare new tree vs old tree at a boundary".
- **Position strategies split into three classes.** Emit-and-forget (M1),
  delta-shift of retained coordinates (M2, M3), and patch-on-edit (M5) vs
  never-stored/derived (M7). The benchmark's R6/R9 surfaces must price
  these separately or reuse wins will be misattributed.
- **Fallback is where several mechanisms hide their true cost.** M2 falls
  back to full parse for any definition-bearing document; M5 shuts down
  reuse entirely under ambiguity/error versions. Fallback frequency
  counters (R0 §10) are the instrument that exposes this.
- **Correctness authorities split into mechanism rules (M5, M8), conservative
  heuristics + test oracles (M3/M4, M7), and totality (M1).** M2 is the
  outlier: its incremental path has no self-equivalence oracle — exactly
  the shape the #22 correctness contract is designed to catch.
